Row-by-row guide

Which layout should I choose?
  • Loom / square stitch: beads sit in straight rows and columns, like the chart you sent. Easiest to read. Great for bracelets and bookmarks on a bead loom.
  • Peyote: every other column sits half a bead lower. Makes a flexible fabric with no loom. Good for bracelets and wrapped tubes.
  • Brick stitch: every other row shifts half a bead sideways. Popular for earrings and shaped motifs like fringe earrings.
Picking bead sizes

Seed beads are sized by a number with “/0”: the bigger the number, the smaller the bead. 11/0 is the most common. Cylinder beads (Miyuki Delica) are very even, so pictures come out crisp; that's why most patterns use them. Round seed beads give a softer, more textured look. Pearls are great for loom or brick work with a few large beads.

How much to buy

The Bead count tab adds 10% to every colour. Some beads are misshapen, some roll away, and dye lots can differ, so buy everything for one project at the same time. Small tubes are often 5–8 g.

Thread and needles
  • Use beading thread (Nymo, KO, One-G) or braided line (FireLine 4–6 lb) for 11/0 beads.
  • Use a size 10 or 12 beading needle for 11/0 beads, and size 13 for 15/0.
  • Work with about an arm's length (1–1.5 m) of thread at a time. Longer thread tangles.
  • Leave a 15–20 cm tail at the start so you can weave it in later.
Using this app while you bead
  • Open the Rows tab. The current row is outlined on the chart and the colours are listed in reading order (“3 Red, 2 White…”).
  • Tap Row done, next after each row. Finished rows fade on the chart, and your place is saved.
  • In peyote, string rows 1 and 2 together at the start, then add one row at a time.
  • Brick stitch is usually worked from the longest row outward. Start your tracker wherever you start.
Drawing moons, stars and other shapes
  • Choose Shape in the toolbar, pick Moon, Star, Heart…, and drag a box on the chart.
  • The shape stays editable until you tap Done. Drag it to move it, change its colour, or use the sliders: moon thickness and tip length, number of star points and how sharp they are, turn, and how many beads sit on the edge.
  • Small shapes can't be perfectly smooth in beads. Stars look best 11 or more beads wide, moons 9 or more. Odd widths (11, 13, 15) keep a star's top point centred on one bead.
  • Outline draws just the edge; Cut out removes beads in that shape, which is handy for cutting a moon out of a filled circle.
  • The app calculates the shape at the beads' real proportions, so a circle stays round even though Delica beads are taller than they are wide.
Drawing shortcuts
  • Drag with one finger to colour many beads. Pinch with two fingers to zoom.
  • Mirror copies what you draw to the other side, which is handy for hearts, flowers and symmetric earrings.
  • Fill colours a whole area of the same colour, like a background.
  • Photo turns a picture into a starting pattern with the number of colours you choose.
Common beginner mistakes
  • Thread tension too loose: pull each stitch snug but not tight enough to buckle the work.
  • Losing your place: use the row tracker and a bead mat so beads don't roll.
  • Too many colours: start with 3–5 colours and a small grid (around 20 × 20).
